wedding videographer

A photograph holds a moment. A film holds the sound of it — your grandmother's voice on the microphone, the song you didn't expect to cry at, and the noise the room made when you walked back down the aisle.

We film the way we shoot stills: from inside the day rather than in front of it. No staged run-throughs, no asking you to do something again for the camera. What ends up in the edit is what actually happened.

Every film is cut and colour-graded by hand. That takes considerably longer than running a preset across the footage, and it's the difference between a video of your wedding and a film you'll sit down and watch again.

The highlight film runs two to four minutes — that's the one you'll send to everybody. The short story film runs four to six, and adds the ceremony and the speeches edited in full, which is what you'll actually want in twenty years when the short version isn't enough.

Film and photography run on the same day, shot by the same team. That matters more than it sounds: two separate suppliers spend the day competing for the same corner of the room, and you spend it repeating yourself to both. One team, one timeline.
